The nine member, bipartisan committee, created under Senate Resolution 476, unanimously passed a 185 page report at its final meeting at the State Capitol. Among the recommendations covering education, public safety, health care, agriculture and entertainment, the report calls for public and private partnerships to develop AI pathways in Georgia K-12 schools and for AI integration plans in higher education. Committee chair Sen. John Albers, R-Roswell, said he wants the recommendations turned into state legislation.

Read the [source](https://senatepress.net/sen-john-albers-announces-completion-of-senate-study-committee-on-artificial-intelligence.html).

The committee's K-12 recommendation, if acted on by lawmakers, would shape how Georgia schools fold AI into science and technology instruction.
