---

The students worked in the Institute for Matter and Systems Biocleanroom under mentor Nik Roeske, using two microfabrication techniques, a metal 3D printer to build a boat-shaped test structure called a 3DBenchy and a separate 3D printer to print a polymer bottle enclosure around it. The finished piece measures about 200 microns long, smaller than a grain of table salt, and the team has submitted it for consideration as a world record.

Read the [source](https://news.research.gatech.edu/2026/10/01/high-school-students-create-microscopic-boat-bottle).

The project gives a concrete, Georgia-based example of how hands-on nanotechnology training can introduce high school students to advanced manufacturing careers.
