University of Georgia researchers incubated 146 alligator eggs collected from seven nests in South Carolina under five fluctuating temperature regimes designed to mimic real nest conditions, then used the results to build a model linking incubation temperature to offspring sex. The study found that at warmer temperatures, a shift of just one or two degrees Fahrenheit could change whether embryos developed as male or female, and that the point at which outcomes swing back toward female, previously estimated near 97 degrees, actually begins around 93 degrees. The work was led by doctoral student Karin Ebey and senior author Benjamin Parrott of UGA's Savannah River Ecology Laboratory and Odum School of Ecology, and was published in the Journal of Experimental Biology.
